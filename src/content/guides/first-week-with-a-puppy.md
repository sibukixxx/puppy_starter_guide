Bringing a puppy home for the first time is exciting — and a little overwhelming. This guide walks you through exactly what to expect in the first seven days, from the drive home to your puppy's first full night of sleep.

Every puppy is different, and this is a general guideline, not medical advice. Talk to your veterinarian about anything specific to your puppy's age, breed or health.

## What to Prepare Before Puppy Comes Home

Have these ready before pickup day:

- Crate sized for your puppy's adult size (with a divider for growing breeds)
- Puppy-safe food and water bowls
- The same food your puppy has been eating (switch gradually if needed)
- A collar, ID tag and a lightweight leash
- Puppy pads or an outdoor potty spot picked out in advance
- A few safe chew toys
- A vet appointment booked for the first week

## The First Day

Keep the first day calm. Limit visitors, keep the house quiet, and let your puppy explore one or two rooms rather than the whole house at once.

- Take your puppy to the potty spot immediately after arriving
- Offer water, then a small meal on their normal schedule
- Introduce the crate as a calm, positive space — not a punishment
- Expect some whining or anxiety; this is normal for a puppy in a new environment

## The First Night

The first night is often the hardest part of the first week.

- Keep the crate near your bed so your puppy can hear and smell you
- Take a potty break right before bedtime, and be ready for at least one nighttime potty trip
- Expect crying — respond calmly, but avoid turning nighttime potty breaks into playtime
- Don't rush to fix "eight hours of sleep" in the first night — it takes time

## Feeding

- Stick to the same food and feeding schedule your breeder or shelter used
- Puppies under 4 months typically need 3–4 meals per day
- Feed at consistent times to make potty schedules predictable
- Avoid table scraps, even as a treat, in the first week

## Potty Training Basics

- Take your puppy out immediately after waking, eating, drinking and playing
- Use the same door and spot every time to build a habit
- Praise or reward immediately after your puppy goes in the right spot
- Expect accidents — they are part of learning, not a sign of failure

For a detailed age-based schedule, see our [Puppy Potty Training Schedule by Age](/guides/puppy-potty-training-schedule/).

## Sleeping

Puppies sleep a lot — often 18–20 hours a day in the first few months. Nap time between meals and play is normal and healthy. If your puppy seems unusually lethargic or won't wake up to eat, contact your vet.

## Crate Introduction

- Leave the crate door open during the day so your puppy can explore it freely
- Feed meals in or near the crate to build a positive association
- Start with short, supervised crate sessions before using it overnight
- Never use the crate as punishment

## Play and Bonding

Short, gentle play sessions help your puppy bond with your family without overstimulation. Watch for signs of tiredness — a puppy that's overtired often becomes bitier and harder to settle.

## Common First-Week Mistakes

- Trying to establish a "perfect" routine on day one
- Letting the puppy free-roam the whole house unsupervised
- Punishing accidents instead of redirecting and rewarding
- Skipping the first vet visit
- Overloading your puppy with visitors, car rides and new experiences all at once

## First Week Checklist

- [ ] Crate, food, bowls, leash and ID tag ready before pickup
- [ ] Quiet, low-key first day at home
- [ ] Potty spot established and used consistently
- [ ] Feeding schedule matches previous routine
- [ ] First vet appointment scheduled
- [ ] Crate introduced as a positive space
- [ ] Nighttime routine in place, even if imperfect
