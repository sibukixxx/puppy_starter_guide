Potty training success comes down to one thing more than any other: taking your puppy out *before* they need to go, on a schedule that matches their age. Below are general guidelines — every puppy is different, and breed, size and health can shift these numbers.

If your puppy is having frequent accidents outside of what's typical for their age, or you notice straining, blood, or unusual frequency, talk to your veterinarian.

## Potty Frequency by Age (General Guideline)

| Age | Typical potty interval |
| --- | --- |
| 8 weeks | Every 1–2 hours |
| 10 weeks | Every 1.5–2.5 hours |
| 12 weeks | Every 2–3 hours |
| 4 months | Every 3–4 hours |
| 6 months | Every 4–6 hours |

A common rule of thumb is "age in months + 1 = hours they can hold it" during the day, though puppies can typically hold it longer overnight while sleeping.

## Signs Your Puppy Needs to Go

Watch for these cues rather than relying only on the clock:

- Sniffing the ground repeatedly
- Circling in one spot
- Whining or pacing near the door
- Suddenly stopping play or waking from a nap
- Standing by the door or their usual potty spot

## Example Daily Schedule (12-Week-Old Puppy)

```
07:00  Wake up → potty immediately
07:15  Breakfast
07:35  Potty
08:00  Play / training session
09:00  Nap
10:30  Potty (right after waking)
11:00  Short walk / play
12:30  Lunch
12:50  Potty
13:15  Nap
15:00  Potty (right after waking)
15:15  Play
17:00  Dinner
17:20  Potty
18:00  Play / training
19:30  Potty
21:00  Final potty before bed
21:15  Bedtime
[Overnight]  1 potty break, if needed
```

This is an example schedule, not a rigid rulebook — adjust the timing to fit your household and your puppy's actual cues.

## Common Potty Training Mistakes

- Waiting for your puppy to "ask" to go out before they've learned how
- Punishing accidents after the fact — puppies don't connect the correction to something that happened minutes earlier
- Inconsistent potty spot or route, which slows down habit-forming
- Removing the crate or confinement too early, before potty habits are reliable
- Skipping the middle-of-the-night break for very young puppies
